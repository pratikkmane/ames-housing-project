Actionable Recommendations

Based on comprehensive analysis of 2,930 homes (2006-2010), here are data-driven recommendations for each stakeholder group.

For Real Estate Investors

1. Buy During Market Troughs

Evidence: 2008-2009 crisis created 20-30% buying opportunity
Action: Monitor economic indicators; buy when prices dip
Expected ROI: Historical recovery suggests 20-30% gains

2. Prioritize Quality Over Size

Evidence: Quality (r=0.80) > Size (r=0.71); Quality adds $32K/point
Action: Target high-quality homes even if smaller
Why: Quality appreciation typically exceeds size appreciation

3. Avoid Over-Renovating Old Homes

Evidence: Age penalty (−$XXX/year) dominates renovation premium
Action: Buy newer homes rather than renovating very old ones
Exception: Cosmetic updates OK; major structural renovations questionable ROI

For Home Sellers

1. List in Late Spring (May-June)

Evidence: $20K seasonal premium May-June vs. Jan-Feb
Action: Prepare home in winter, list by May 1st
Timeline: - March: Repairs, staging - April: Professional photos, listing prep - May 1-15: Launch listing - June-July: Accept offers

2. Focus Quality Improvements

Evidence: Each quality point = +$32K; highest ROI feature
Action Priority: 1. Kitchen/bathroom upgrades (visible quality) 2. Fresh paint, modern fixtures 3. Curb appeal 4. NOT: Adding square footage (lower ROI)

3. Price Using Model + Seasonal Adjustment

Evidence: Model predicts ±$39K; season adds ±$10K
Formula:

Base Price (from model)
+ Seasonal adjustment (+$15K if June, −$10K if Jan)
+ Location premium (if premium neighborhood)
= Listing Price

For Home Buyers

1. Shop in Winter (January-February)

Evidence: Winter discount of $10K-15K; 50% fewer competing buyers
Action: Browse in Dec-Jan, make offers in Jan-Feb
Negotiation power: Sellers more motivated in winter

2. Hunt for Value in Quality-7 Homes

Evidence: Quality 7-8 gap is smaller than 8-9; diminishing returns at top
Sweet spot: Quality 7 homes in good neighborhoods
Why: Get 90% of the benefits at 70% of the price

3. Don’t Overpay for Central Air

Evidence: AC adds $85K in simple comparison, but only $17K in full model
Interpretation: AC itself isn’t worth $85K; it’s correlated with newer, nicer homes
Action: Negotiate; AC installation costs only $5K-7K

For Property Developers

1. Target 1,800-2,200 Sq Ft Range

Evidence: Sweet spot for price-per-square-foot efficiency
Why: Below 1,800 = too small for families; above 2,200 = diminishing returns
Build: 1,900-2,000 sq ft as optimal

2. Emphasize Quality Over Size

Evidence: Quality rating has 2x the correlation with price vs. size
Investment priority: 1. Premium finishes (quality 8+) 2. Modern amenities (AC, garage, bathrooms) 3. THEN size additions

3. Avoid Building Luxury Spec Homes

Evidence: Model less accurate above $300K (heteroscedasticity)
Risk: High-end market more volatile and unpredictable
Safer bet: Quality 7-8 homes at $200-250K (sweet spot)

Risk Warnings

All Stakeholders Should Know:

⚠ Time Period Limitation: Analysis based on 2006-2010; market conditions differ today
⚠ Location Missing: Model doesn’t include neighborhood; adjust for location
⚠ Crisis Bias: Data includes financial crisis; not representative of normal markets
⚠ Ames-Specific: College town dynamics may not apply elsewhere

Summary Decision Matrix

Goal Best Timing Key Features Expected Outcome
Buy for investment Jan-Feb, market dips Quality 7-8, 1,800-2,000 sq ft 20-30% ROI over cycle
Sell for max price May-June, strong economy Quality 8+, 2,000+ sq ft, AC +$20K vs. poor timing
Build spec home Year-round Quality 8, 1,900 sq ft, AC, garage Reliable $220-250K sale
Buy personal home Jan-Feb, weak market Quality 7, AC, newer Save $20-30K vs. peak

Bottom Line: Timing and quality matter more than most people think. Data shows $20K swings from timing alone and $32K per quality point—both larger than typical renovation ROI.
